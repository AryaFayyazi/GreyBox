Labels, not access

What a black box reveals about its hidden state, and how to monitor closed agents.

Arya Fayyazi · preprint in preparation. The full preregistration record is on the evidence page.

Summary

Safety monitors that read a model's internals need its weights, but the most capable agents expose only text. We ask what outputs reveal about a model's hidden state, and what monitoring a closed agent actually requires.

A model's internals are computed from the text it has read. So they cannot carry more information about a future violation than the full transcript does. What white-box access buys is narrower:

  • Seeing hidden tokens. After an agent's private reasoning its action is nearly fixed. Internals read at that moment beat every monitor that reads before it, and a monitor that reads the proposed action recovers the advantage (T2).
  • A good representation, which needs labels. Without labels, a 70B judge scoring resampled actions loses to a probe (T3). With labels, an open reader model with a linear probe, reading only the transcript, matches the agent's own internals (T4, T5), transfers to other agents including a closed frontier one (T6, T8b), and to an external benchmark (T7).
  • The policy is the limit. A bag-of-words classifier on the same labels matches the reader in distribution but fails on new tasks and benchmarks (T9). A policy absent from training transfers when it resembles a trained one (T12), and can be missed when it does not, until a few dozen labelled examples are added (T10).

For chat, a model's answers to side-branch questions recover its behavioural state on models with known internals (a compiled program, Othello-GPT, eight open chat models) and transfer to Claude. As a safety signal they tie a strong graded moderator at forecasting refusals, and in combination with one they double the precision of an alarm for harmful requests that get answered.

How the evidence was produced

  • Every confirmatory test was written down and hash-locked before its data was collected or scored; deviations and failures are reported.
  • Agents: Gemma-4 and gpt-oss (open), Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6-luna (closed, through their public APIs) on CAPE-Bench; AgentDojo for prompt injection.
  • Uncertainty: cluster bootstrap over scenarios (agents) or conversations (chat), 1,000–2,000 resamples.
