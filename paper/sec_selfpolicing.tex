\subsection{Self-policing: the agent's own API as the monitor}
\label{sec:selfpolicing}
A monitor of a closed agent can also use only the agent's API. Before a proposed call runs, we ask the agent in a side branch, which its conversation never sees, whether the call complies with its policy (four sampled answers). A flagged call goes to review (\emph{gate}), or is replaced by a fresh draw of the agent, with review after four flags (\emph{S4}). No other model is used and nothing is trained.

\begin{proposition}[prevention law]\label{prop:prevention}
At a state $x$ let $R$ be the agent's probability of a violating call, and let the audit flag a violating call with probability $\tau$ and a safe one with probability $\varphi$, independently across draws. With $f=R\tau+(1-R)\varphi$ and at most $M$ draws, a violation executes with probability $R(1-\tau)\,(1-f^M)/(1-f)$ and the step goes to review with probability $f^M$.
\end{proposition}
All terms are measurable through the API: $R$ by resampling, $\tau$ and $\varphi$ by auditing. More draws turn reviews into executed actions at a small cost in safety.

\textbf{An open agent polices itself (preregistered).} Gemma-4 flags 96.6\% of its own violating calls when asked, and 11.1\% of its safe calls (development data). On 333 unseen scenario instances with two seeds each, violating episodes fall from 27.9\% to 4.95\% with the gate ($-82\%$ [76, 88]) and to 4.35\% with S4 ($-84\%$ [78, 90]), and task success rises from 33.5\% to 43.7\% and 38.7\%: a blocked or replaced violating call steers the agent to the allowed route. S4 needed 0.404 reviews per episode, just above its preregistered limit of 0.40, so that endpoint failed.

\textbf{It is verification, not self-knowledge.} gpt-oss flags only 37\% of its own violations. In a cross-audit, each auditor does better on Gemma-4's calls than on gpt-oss's, and Gemma-4 is the better auditor; the self $\times$ agent interaction is $+0.03$. Self-policing works when the agent is a competent judge of the policy and its violations are slips: where Gemma-4 violated, its own resampled violation rate had median 0.31.

\textbf{Two kinds of violation self-audit cannot see.} Claude Haiku~5.5 flags 5\% of its own violations. They are (i) production restarts without approval, a misreading of rule 4 that Claude does flag when asked to check each rule in turn (0.85 vs 0.06), and (ii) test sends to a mailing list whose thousands of external members appear only after sending. The second kind is decided by a fact that is not yet in the transcript, so no judge of the transcript can see it (\cref{cor:action}). It can be prevented only by making the agent check first.
