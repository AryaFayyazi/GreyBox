\subsection{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}
A language model reads a context $x$ (tokens) and reaches an internal state $s(x)$, a deterministic function of the tokens. An outside observer sees only outputs: sampled text and, on some APIs, the log-probabilities of the next token. A \emph{battery} $Q=(q_1,\dots,q_m)$ of yes/no questions is asked in \emph{side branches} (\cref{fig:method}): each question is appended to a copy of $x$ in its own call, the copy is then discarded, so the model's real conversation never sees it, and the answers are independent given $x$. The \emph{observable state} is $\Psi_Q(x)=\big(P(\textsc{yes}\mid x\oplus q_j)\big)_j$, and the answers follow the product law $P_x=\bigotimes_j\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\Psi_{q_j}(x))$. Where a model is open, its hidden state $h(x)\in\mathbb{R}^d$ at a layer is the ground truth that we test against.

\subsection{What cannot be read: a gauge symmetry}
\begin{proposition}[gauge]\label{prop:gauge}
Take a pre-norm residual network with RMS normalisation, with the normalisation gains folded into the adjacent weights. For every orthogonal $G$ on the residual stream, the network whose embedding, write matrices and write biases are multiplied by $G$ on the left, and whose read matrices and unembedding are multiplied by $G^{\top}$ on the right, has hidden states $G h$ and the same output distribution on every input. At a layer that is read only through linear maps, the same holds for every invertible $G$, with $G^{-1}$ in place of $G^{\top}$.
\end{proposition}
No estimator that uses only outputs can recover hidden \emph{coordinates} beyond this group (computational invariance; \citealp{ashkboos2024slicegpt}). Distances and angles between states are invariant under the orthogonal group, so the \emph{geometry} of the states is the natural target. Post-block normalisation (Gemma-3, OLMo-3) restricts $G$ to the maps that commute with its gains (\cref{app:proofs}).

\subsection{What can be read: the observable geometry}
Let $d_Q(x,x')$ be the Fisher--Rao geodesic distance between the answer laws $P_x$ and $P_{x'}$.
\begin{theorem}[observable geometry]\label{thm:geometry}
(a) \emph{Exact flat coordinates.} With $\vartheta_j=2\arcsin\sqrt{\Psi_{q_j}}$, $d_Q(x,x')^2=\sum_j\big(\vartheta_j(x)-\vartheta_j(x')\big)^2$.
(b) \emph{Invariance and uniqueness.} $d_Q$ depends on the outputs only, so it is gauge invariant; up to a constant, the Fisher metric is the only Riemannian metric on the simplex over $\{0,1\}^m$ invariant under Markov morphisms (\v{C}encov), and $d_Q$ is its geodesic distance on the independent answer laws.
(c) \emph{Relation to the hidden state.} If $\Psi_j=\sigma(a_j(s))$ with smooth $a_j$, then for nearby states $d_Q^2=\Delta^{\top}G_Q(s)\,\Delta+O(|\Delta|^3)$, with the pulled-back Fisher metric $G_Q=\sum_j\Psi_j(1-\Psi_j)\nabla a_j\nabla a_j^{\top}$.
(d) \emph{Estimation from sampled answers.} Let $\mu_n(p)=\E\,\hat\vartheta$ for the Anscombe angle $\hat\vartheta=2\arcsin\sqrt{(k+3/8)/(n+3/4)}$, $k\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)$; $\mu_n$ is known, strictly increasing, and $\mu_n\to\vartheta$. For two independent halves $A,B$ of $n$ samples each, $\hat D^2(x,x')=\sum_j\big(\hat\vartheta^A_j(x)-\hat\vartheta^A_j(x')\big)\big(\hat\vartheta^B_j(x)-\hat\vartheta^B_j(x')\big)$ satisfies $\E\hat D^2=\sum_j\big(\mu_n(\Psi_j(x))-\mu_n(\Psi_j(x'))\big)^2$ exactly, for every $\Psi$, when every cell has the same $n$.
\end{theorem}
Parts (a)--(c) are classical information geometry \citep{amari2016information}; part (d) is ours. Part (a) is a change of variables: nothing is fit, and classical scaling of $d_Q$ gives coordinates that are exact up to a rigid motion. Part (b) says these are the only natural coordinates; \citet{picozzi2026information} study the same object for next-token laws. Part (c) ties them to the hidden state: agreement should rise as more questions give $G_Q$ more rank. Part (d) makes the geometry measurable on any chat API, at $m\cdot n$ one-token calls per context (exactness needs the same $n$ in every cell and answers that are yes or no; \cref{app:proofs}).

\textbf{Checking a closed model's reconstruction.} A closed model's hidden states cannot be seen, so how well its answer geometry matches its own hidden geometry cannot be measured directly. It can be estimated, because hidden geometries share a common part across models (\cref{sec:geom-results}).
\begin{proposition}[triangulation]\label{prop:triang}
On the vector of all context-pair distances, let the hidden geometry of model $m$ be $H_m=\lambda_mC+e_m$ and the answer geometry of answerer $a$ be $A_a=\kappa_aC+f_a$ (standardized; the parts $e_m$, $f_a$ uncorrelated with $C$ and across models). Then $\rho(H_a,H_b)=\lambda_a\lambda_b$ and $\rho(A_a,H_b)=\kappa_a\lambda_b$ for $a\neq b$, and the unseen agreement is $\rho(A_c,H_c)=\kappa_c\lambda_c+\rho(f_c,e_c)$. Open models identify the $\lambda_b$; a closed model's answers against them identify $\kappa_c$.
\end{proposition}
With no own-model excess ($\rho(f_c,e_c)=0$) and $\lambda_c$ in the open models' range, $\kappa_c\cdot\mathrm{median}(\lambda)$ estimates the closed model's own agreement; leaving each open model out tests the estimator.

Where an API returns full log-probabilities, the final state's geometry is recovered exactly, not estimated (\cref{thm:exact}, \cref{app:exact}, after \citet[App.~H]{carlini2024stealing}); closed APIs do not return them, so the grey box does not use this.

\subsection{From the geometry to monitoring agents}
\label{sec:agents-theory}
\textbf{Setting.} At step $t$ of an episode the operator holds the visible tokens $V_t$ (instructions, task, earlier calls and their results) and sees the proposed call $A_t$; $Y\in\{0,1\}$ marks a policy violation. A monitor scores $(V_t,A_t)$ and holds the call when the score passes a cut; its budget $\beta$ is the share of calls it holds.
\begin{corollary}[the transcript suffices]\label{cor:action}
Let $Y=g(A_t,V_t,W)$, where the world facts $W$ satisfy $W\indep Z^-_t\mid V_t$ and $Z^-_t$ are all tokens before the call, hidden ones included. Then $P(Y\mid Z^-_t,A_t)=P(Y\mid V_t,A_t)$: no monitor of the agent's internals has a lower Bayes risk than the best monitor of $(V_t,A_t)$.
\end{corollary}
The premise is about the label, and it is testable: it holds when facts shown in the transcript decide the violation (CAPE-Bench is built this way, and so are most $\tau^2$ writes), and it fails when a fact the agent never checked decides it, where no monitor of the transcript, the agent's internals included, can see it (\cref{sec:carry}). Where it holds, a monitor needs the transcript and the call, not the weights.
\begin{proposition}[the forecast is the agent's policy]\label{prop:selfforecast}
Before step $t$ the agent acts by $A_t\sim\pi(\cdot\mid V_t,Z^-_t)$; let $\bar\pi(a\mid V_t)$ be this policy averaged over the hidden states consistent with $V_t$, and $r(a,V_t)=P(Y\mid a,V_t)$. The Bayes forecast is $f=\sum_a\bar\pi(a\mid V_t)\,r(a,V_t)$. A reader that forecasts with another policy $\pi'$, $f'=\sum_a\pi'(a\mid V_t)\,r(a,V_t)$, has squared-loss excess risk
\[ \E(Y-f')^2-\E(Y-f)^2=\E\,\Delta^2\le\E\,\|\bar\pi-\pi'\|_1^2, \]
with $\Delta=\sum_a(\bar\pi-\pi')(a\mid V_t)\,r(a,V_t)$. The excess is zero once the call $A_t$ is visible.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$f=\E[Y\mid V_t]$, so $\E(Y-f')^2=\E(Y-f)^2+\E(f-f')^2$; and $|f-f'|\le\|\bar\pi-\pi'\|_1$ since $0\le r\le1$. Given $A_t$, both forecasts equal $r(A_t,V_t)$.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[reading the policy by sampling]\label{cor:rollout}
Let $a_1,\dots,a_M$ be next actions drawn from the agent in side branches, so $a_k\sim\bar\pi(\cdot\mid V_t)$ i.i.d., and $\hat f=\frac1M\sum_k r(a_k,V_t)$. Then $\E(Y-\hat f)^2-\E(Y-f)^2=\E\,\Var_{\bar\pi}[r(a,V_t)]/M\le 1/(4M)$.
\end{corollary}
The corollary is elementary (a Monte Carlo mean); its point is that sampling the agent reads its own policy, and with it the Bayes forecast; a self-report or another model's draws are readers with another policy and pay the excess of \cref{prop:selfforecast}. Since $\E(Y-q)^2-\E(Y-f)^2=\E(f-q)^2$ for any forecast $q$ of the context alone, the draws estimate a text monitor's loss with no label: $\overline{(\hat f-q)^2}-\overline{\hat f(1-\hat f)}/(M-1)$. In practice $r$ is not known: we replace it by the read-out of each drawn call, the agent's own answers at that call.
So at the call any capable reader can match the agent (\cref{cor:action}); before it, a reader loses exactly as much as its policy differs from the agent's on violating actions. Another model's answers can forecast well only where it would act like the agent (tested in \cref{sec:forecast}).

\textbf{The observable state at the decision.} At a proposed call we ask the agent, through its own API, $m$ yes/no questions about the call in side branches. Their angles $\vartheta_t\in[0,\pi]^m$ are its observable state, in the coordinates of \cref{thm:geometry}. The score is a fixed read-out, the mean angle in each question's violation direction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:readout} s_t=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sigma_j\,\vartheta_{tj},\qquad \sigma_j\in\{-1,+1\}. \end{equation}
Here $\sigma_j=-1$ for the question ``does it comply'' and $+1$ for the others. The read-out is one projection of the observable geometry. The geometry alone also gives a monitor: a call's Fisher--Rao distance from the agent's typical state, $g_t=\|\vartheta_t-\tilde\vartheta\|$ with $\tilde\vartheta$ the median of the agent's own unlabelled calls (\cref{thm:geometry}). It needs no sign: rewording a question to its negation maps $\vartheta_j$ to $\pi-\vartheta_j$ and leaves $g_t$ unchanged. No label is used and nothing is fit. Self-policing is the one-question case: hold the call when the agent's own answers say that it does not comply.
\begin{proposition}[a label-free ceiling]\label{prop:snrceil}
Let $N$ be the sampling-noise variance of $s_t$ and $\mathrm{SNR}=\mathrm{Var}(s_t)/N-1$; neither uses a label. If the noise is independent of $Y$ and $s_t$ is Gaussian with equal variance in the two classes, then for a violation rate $p$,
$\mathrm{AUROC}(s_t)\le\Phi\big(\sqrt{\mathrm{SNR}^+/(2p(1-p))}\big)$, with $\mathrm{SNR}^+=\max(\mathrm{SNR},0)$; at a true $\mathrm{SNR}\le0$ the read-out cannot rank violations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
With class gap $\delta$ and within-class variance $\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^2\ge N$, $\mathrm{Var}(s_t)=\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^2+p(1-p)\delta^2$, so $p(1-p)\delta^2\le\mathrm{SNR}\cdot N\le\mathrm{SNR}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^2$; and $\mathrm{AUROC}=\Phi(\delta/\sqrt2\sigma_{\mathrm{in}})$.
\end{proof}
In practice we use the nominal noise $\frac1{m^2}\sum_j(n_j+\frac12)^{-1}$ for $n_j$ parsed answers; it overstates the noise near $P(\textsc{yes})\in\{0,1\}$, so the nominal SNR is conservative: a high value supports a setting, and a low value flags it without ruling it out. More answers lower the noise and raise the ceiling.

\textbf{The cut.} A cut on the read-out is set on the agent's own unlabelled traffic and then frozen (\cref{prop:frozen}); a state-space model of the angles across steps is in \cref{app:agents}.

\textbf{Metrics.} For geometries: Spearman correlation between two distance matrices over all pairs of contexts (no fitted parameter). For monitors: the \emph{within-stratum AUROC}, the AUROC over pairs of a violating and a safe call from the same stratum, where a stratum is a template (a CAPE-Bench task family), a tool, or an AgentDojo suite, and, where stated, the call's \emph{position} (its ordinal among the episode's calls); and the share of violations caught when exactly $\beta$ of the actions are escalated. Intervals are 95\% percentile intervals from a bootstrap over contexts, scenarios or episodes. Proofs are in \cref{app:proofs}.
