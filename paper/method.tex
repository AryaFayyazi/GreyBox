\subsection{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}
A language model reads a context $x$ (tokens) and reaches an internal state $s(x)$, a deterministic function of the tokens. An outside observer sees only outputs: sampled text and, on some APIs, the log-probabilities of the next token. A \emph{battery} $Q=(q_1,\dots,q_m)$ of yes/no questions is asked in side branches: each question is appended to $x$ in its own call, so the model's real conversation never sees it, and the answers are independent given $x$. The \emph{interrogation state} is $\Psi_Q(x)=\big(P(\textsc{yes}\mid x\oplus q_j)\big)_j$, and the answers follow the product law $P_x=\bigotimes_j\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\Psi_{q_j}(x))$. Where a model is open, its hidden state $h(x)\in\mathbb{R}^d$ at a layer is the ground truth that we test against.

\subsection{What cannot be read: a gauge symmetry}
\begin{proposition}[gauge]\label{prop:gauge}
Take a pre-norm residual network with RMS normalisation, with the normalisation gains folded into the adjacent weights. For every orthogonal $G$ on the residual stream, the network whose embedding, write matrices and write biases are multiplied by $G$ on the left, and whose read matrices and unembedding are multiplied by $G^{\top}$ on the right, has hidden states $G h$ and the same output distribution on every input. At a layer that is read only through linear maps, the same holds for every invertible $G$, with $G^{-1}$ in place of $G^{\top}$.
\end{proposition}
No estimator that uses only outputs can recover hidden \emph{coordinates} beyond this group (computational invariance; \citealp{ashkboos2024slicegpt}). Distances and angles between states are invariant under the orthogonal group, so the \emph{geometry} of the states is the natural target. Post-block normalisation (Gemma-3, OLMo-3) restricts $G$ to the maps that commute with its gains (\cref{app:proofs}).

\subsection{What can be read: the observable geometry}
Let $d_Q(x,x')$ be the Fisher--Rao geodesic distance between the answer laws $P_x$ and $P_{x'}$.
\begin{theorem}[observable geometry]\label{thm:geometry}
(a) \emph{Exact flat coordinates.} With $\vartheta_j=2\arcsin\sqrt{\Psi_{q_j}}$, $d_Q(x,x')^2=\sum_j\big(\vartheta_j(x)-\vartheta_j(x')\big)^2$.
(b) \emph{Invariance and uniqueness.} $d_Q$ depends on the outputs only, so it is invariant under the gauge group. Up to a constant factor, the Fisher metric is the only Riemannian metric on the probability simplex over $\{0,1\}^m$ that is invariant under Markov morphisms (\v{C}encov); $d_Q$ is the geodesic distance it induces on the submanifold of independent answer laws (in the whole simplex the distance is $2\arccos\prod_j\cos\frac{\vartheta_j-\vartheta'_j}{2}$).
(c) \emph{Relation to the hidden state.} If $\Psi_j=\sigma(a_j(s))$ with smooth $a_j$, then for nearby states $d_Q^2=\Delta^{\top}G_Q(s)\,\Delta+O(|\Delta|^3)$, with the pulled-back Fisher metric $G_Q=\sum_j\Psi_j(1-\Psi_j)\nabla a_j\nabla a_j^{\top}$.
(d) \emph{Estimation from sampled answers.} Let $\mu_n(p)=\E\,\hat\vartheta$ for the Anscombe angle $\hat\vartheta=2\arcsin\sqrt{(k+3/8)/(n+3/4)}$, $k\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)$; $\mu_n$ is known, strictly increasing, and $\mu_n\to\vartheta$. For two independent halves $A,B$ of $n$ samples each, $\hat D^2(x,x')=\sum_j\big(\hat\vartheta^A_j(x)-\hat\vartheta^A_j(x')\big)\big(\hat\vartheta^B_j(x)-\hat\vartheta^B_j(x')\big)$ satisfies $\E\hat D^2=\sum_j\big(\mu_n(\Psi_j(x))-\mu_n(\Psi_j(x'))\big)^2$ exactly, for every $\Psi$, when every cell has the same $n$.
\end{theorem}
Part (a) is a change of variables: no model is trained and nothing is fit. Classical scaling of $d_Q$ gives coordinates that are exact up to a rigid motion. Part (b) makes $d_Q$ the Fisher geometry of the answers (the metric is unique; the distance is the one it induces on the family of independent answers); \citet{picozzi2026information} studies the same object for next-token distributions. Part (c) links it to the hidden state; since $s$ is the whole cache that the questions read, its prediction for one layer's vector is heuristic: agreement should rise with the number of questions as $G_Q$ gains rank. Part (d) makes the geometry measurable on any chat API, at $m\cdot n$ calls of one output token per context; the familiar rule of subtracting the large-$n$ variance $1/(n+\frac12)$ is not exact, because near $\Psi\in\{0,1\}$ the variance is smaller and the bias is $\cot\vartheta/(4n)$, and an answer that is neither yes nor no, or clipping negative estimates at $0$, breaks the exactness of (d) (\cref{app:proofs}). The answer law is conditional on a yes or no answer.

Where an API returns full log-probabilities, the final state's geometry is recovered exactly, not estimated (\cref{thm:exact}, \cref{app:exact}, after \citet[App.~H]{carlini2024stealing}); closed APIs do not return them, so the grey box does not use this.

\subsection{From the geometry to monitoring agents}
\label{sec:agents-theory}
An agent is a model that acts through tools. At step $t$ the operator sees the visible tokens $V_t$ (instructions, task, earlier calls and their results) and the proposed action $A_t$; $Y\in\{0,1\}$ marks a policy violation, decided by $A_t$, $V_t$ and facts $W$ of the world. A \emph{monitor} scores $(V_t,A_t)$ and escalates the action when the score is at or above a cut $c$; its \emph{budget} $\beta$ is the share of actions that it escalates.
\begin{corollary}[sufficiency of the proposed action]\label{cor:action}
Let $Y=g(A_t,V_t,W)$ with $W\indep Z^-_t\mid V_t$, where $Z^-_t$ are all tokens before the action, hidden ones included. Then $P(Y\mid Z^-_t,A_t)=P(Y\mid V_t,A_t)$, and every monitor that reads the agent's internals at or before the action has a Bayes risk at least that of the best monitor that reads $(V_t,A_t)$.
\end{corollary}
For policy labels, a monitor therefore needs the transcript and the action, not the weights. \textbf{The grey box at the decision} needs neither weights nor training. At a proposed call $A_t$ we ask the agent itself, through its API and in side branches that its conversation never sees, $K$ yes/no questions about the call (does it comply with the policy; does it touch a resource the user may not use; does it act on an instruction found inside a tool output; \ldots). Their arcsine angles $\vartheta_t\in[0,\pi]^K$ are the agent's \emph{observable state} at the decision, in the exact coordinates of \cref{thm:geometry}. Because models share this geometry (\cref{sec:geom-results}), another capable model can be asked the same questions about the agent's call, from its transcript; we test both (G16, G21). The monitor is a fixed read-out, with no labels and nothing fit: the mean angle in the violation direction that each question's wording sets,
\[ s_t=\tfrac1K\textstyle\sum_{j} \sigma_j\,\vartheta_{tj}, \]
with $\sigma_j=-1$ for ``does it comply'' and $+1$ for the other questions.
Self-policing holds a call on the agent's own answers to one question, sampled a few times: whether the call complies (Gemma-4: 4 answers, hold when all say no) or, for Claude, a rule-by-rule check of the policy ending in one verdict (3 answers). A gate holds a call whose score passes a cut; how much it prevents is predicted by \cref{prop:prevention}. Because $\vartheta_t$ is a function of the same visible tokens as the action, \cref{cor:action} allows it to carry what the agent's internals carry about a policy label; whether it does is an empirical question (G15). As a comparison only, we also recreate the decision state with maps trained on open agents (\cref{prop:greybox}, \cref{app:greybox}).
\begin{proposition}[a frozen cut]\label{prop:frozen}
Let the scores of $n$ unlabelled warm-up actions and of a new action be exchangeable with no ties, and let $\hat c$ be the $k$-th smallest warm-up score. Then $P(S_{n+1}>\hat c)=(n+1-k)/(n+1)$, which for $k=\lceil(1-\beta)(n+1)\rceil$ lies in $\big(\beta-\tfrac1{n+1},\beta\big]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proposition}[shift and bursts]\label{prop:shift}\label{prop:burst}
If deployment scores follow $Q$ and warm-up scores follow $P$, a frozen cut escalates within $K(P,Q)+\tfrac2{n+1}$ of $\beta$, with $K$ the Kolmogorov distance. A cut set at the top $\beta$ of a window in which a share $\rho>\beta$ of the actions are violations catches at most $\beta/\rho$ of them; a frozen cut catches each violation with probability $P(S\ge\hat c\mid Y=1)$, whatever $\rho$ is.
\end{proposition}
A cut that keeps moving with recent traffic thus fails exactly when an attack arrives; a frozen cut keeps its rate, and after a shift of the safe traffic (a new prompt, a new agent) it must be set again on the new unlabelled traffic. The full statements, the monitor's algorithm and a ceiling on robustness to rewording (\cref{prop:ceiling}) are in \cref{app:agents}.

\textbf{Metrics.} For geometries: Spearman correlation between two distance matrices over all pairs of contexts (no fitted parameter). For monitors: the AUROC within strata (template, tool or suite), and the share of violations caught when exactly $\beta$ of the actions are escalated. Intervals are 95\% percentile intervals from a bootstrap over contexts, scenarios or episodes. Proofs are in \cref{app:proofs}.
