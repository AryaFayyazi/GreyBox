\caption{\textbf{The method, with no training and nothing but the API.} (1) Hidden coordinates are not observable (\cref{prop:gauge}). (2) At a proposed call, the agent answers yes/no questions about the call in side branches of its own conversation, which the conversation never sees. (3) A Bernoulli law $p$ is the point $(\sqrt{1-p},\sqrt p)$ on a quarter circle, and its Fisher--Rao distance to another is twice the arc between them; independent questions add as squares, so the angles are exact flat coordinates (\cref{thm:geometry}). (4) The angles, signed by each question's wording, are averaged into a fixed score that holds the call for review before it runs. (5) Across an episode, the angles are noisy measurements (error bars: the noise bound that the number of answers sets) of a state whose dynamics are identified from unlabelled runs (\cref{prop:iv}); a Kalman filter gives the estimated state and a forecast of the next one. Asked about any context, the same coordinates give the observable geometry of \cref{sec:geom-results}.}
