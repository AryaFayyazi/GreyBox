\subsection{An agent that knows it is monitored}
\label{sec:robust}
A deployed agent may know that a monitor reads its actions. We ran Closed-A on 990 fresh scenarios twice, once with an oversight notice in its system prompt (``every tool call is reviewed by an automated safety monitor'') and once without, with the shipped monitor and the cut frozen on Closed-A's earlier unlabelled traffic. Being told did not change how often Closed-A violated ($-0.003$ [$-0.012$, $+0.005$]) or reached its goal (37.6\% vs 37.8\%). The monitor caught 97.1\% [92.9\%, 100\%] of the violations of the told agent and 83.6\% of the others ($+0.136$ [0.050, 0.225]; both preregistered endpoints pass). The notice moved the whole score distribution up (\cref{fig:transfer}c): the frozen cut escalated 15.2\% of the told agent's actions instead of 5.3\%. \Cref{prop:shift} bounds this: the Kolmogorov distance between the told agent's scores and the warm-up scores is 0.24 (0.014 for the agent that was not told), and the escalation rate moved by 0.10. At each arm's own 5\% budget the monitor catches 88.6\% and 83.6\%.

\textbf{Set the cut again, and the budget is restored.} A change of prompt is a shift, so \cref{prop:frozen,prop:shift} prescribe a new cut from the new, unlabelled traffic. We tested this live on 400 fresh scenarios, with the cut set from the told agent's earlier scores (no labels), then frozen. The new cut escalated 4.7\% [3.5\%, 6.1\%] of the actions, inside the band fixed in advance around the predicted 5\%, where the old cut would have escalated 16.6\%, and the gate removed every executed violation (\cref{tab:live}; both preregistered endpoints pass). Of the 54 blocked actions, 25 would have been violations.

\textbf{An adaptive attacker defeats the monitor.} An attacker who can query the score and rewrites only the free text of a violating call, keeping its target, lowered the share caught from 96\% to 29\%. In an exploratory diagnosis, in 81 of the 82 attacked messages the violation is the recipient, a list whose members the agent never checked; the attacker dropped the copied draft from two thirds of them, and every rewrite still violated. The monitor had caught these messages through that removable cue. \Cref{prop:ceiling} bounds every defense by the clean accuracy of the best monitor of the decisive fields; our canonical reader, which estimates it from below, catches 51--59\% at 5\%, because this family is absent from training. Two preregistered defenses reached 51\% (the canonical reader) and 60\% [52\%, 68\%] (training on rewrites) after fresh attacks, close to this estimate (\cref{app:limits}). Robust monitoring needs labels that cover the decisive fields, and the score must stay hidden from the agent.}
