\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Battery design from black-box answers}
\label{alg:battery}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE contexts $x_1..x_N$; pool of questions $\mathcal Q$; answers $a(x,q)\in\{0,1\}$ from the model's outputs only
\STATE $C\gets$ one class holding all contexts; $Q\gets()$
\LOOP
  \STATE $q^\star\gets\arg\max_{q\in\mathcal Q}$ $\#$classes of $C$ refined by $a(\cdot,q)$
  \STATE \textbf{if} $q^\star$ splits no class \textbf{then return} $Q$ \COMMENT{\cref{prop:battery}}
  \STATE append $q^\star$ to $Q$; $C\gets C$ refined by $a(\cdot,q^\star)$
\ENDLOOP
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

% Experimental details: every value here is the value in the code (scripts/, src/graybox/) and the preregistrations.
\textbf{Agents.} All agents run CAPE-Bench's mock environment for at most 20 turns, with one tool call per turn.
\begin{itemize}
\item Open agents, served by vLLM, at most 4{,}096 new tokens per turn:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Gemma-4 (26B-A4B): temperature 1.0, top-$p$ 0.95, top-$k$ 64, thinking on.
  \item gpt-oss-20b: temperature 1.0, top-$p$ 1.0, reasoning effort ``medium''.
  \end{itemize}
  The episodes of the three additional open agents come from the released CAPE-Bench rollouts.
\item Closed agents use their public tool-use APIs:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Closed-A: thinking disabled, effort ``low'', at most 1{,}024 tokens per turn.
  \item GPT-6-luna: reasoning effort ``none'', no parallel tool calls, at most 2{,}048 tokens per turn.
  \end{itemize}
  The shared spend ledgers record \$24.78 of API use in total (Anthropic \$19.86, OpenAI \$4.92), including tests of the released tool and a 40-episode pilot (\$0.10) of Closed-A on AgentDojo that was not analysed.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Units.} A unit is one proposed action that is not a read, after the step that reveals the deciding fact. Each
episode contributes its actions up to and including its first violation. All violations are kept. Safe actions are
subsampled at random with seed 0, so selection depends only on the past.

\textbf{Reader input.} The reader prompt holds four parts:
\begin{itemize}
\item the operating instructions;
\item the user task;
\item each earlier call with its result (results cut to 2{,}000 characters);
\item the proposed action.
\end{itemize}
A context of 30{,}000 characters or more keeps its first 6{,}000 and last 24{,}000 characters. The prompt goes through
Qwen3-8B's chat template with thinking off and is cut to its last 12{,}000 tokens. The feature is the residual stream at
the last prompt token, at hidden-state index 32 (the output of block 31 of 36), in bf16.

\textbf{Monitor.} The monitor standardises the features, keeps the top 256 principal components, and fits a logistic
regression with $C=0.05$. It is trained on Gemma-4's CAPE-Bench steps only (17{,}623; development and fresh). The
review cut has no labels. Offline (\cref{tab:closed}), it sends exactly 5\% of the agent's actions in the test set, the
highest-scored, to review (actions tied at the cut count in proportion). Live, it was the 95th percentile of
the agent's earlier unlabelled actions, then frozen; the tool freezes it after the first 500. A rolling cut fails under
bursts of similar actions.

\textbf{Replica.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Target:} the agent's residual stream at its end-of-reasoning token, at 5 depths (block $\mathrm{round}(nf)-1$, $f\in\{0.5,0.62,0.72,0.82,0.9\}$). It is standardised and reduced to 128 principal components, all fit on development.
\item \textbf{Map:} ridge regression, with $\alpha\in\{1,10,10^2,10^3,10^4\}$ chosen by 5-fold cross-validation grouped by scenario.
\item \textbf{Probe on the true state:} standardise, then logistic regression with $C=0.05$, on the 128 true components.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Text baseline.} TF-IDF of the context and of the action, kept as two separate blocks:
\begin{itemize}
\item word 1- and 2-grams, minimum document frequency 3, at most 200{,}000 features each, sublinear term frequency;
\item logistic regression with $C\in\{0.1,1,10\}$, chosen by 5-fold cross-validation grouped by scenario.
\end{itemize}
The browser tool runs the same model. On 302 real agent steps it gives the same text as Python, and its held-out AUROC is 0.9887 (Python 0.9886).

\textbf{Statistics.}
\begin{itemize}
\item AUROC is computed within strata (template and tool, or suite) and averaged with weights by the number of positive--negative pairs.
\item Intervals are percentile intervals from 1{,}000 bootstrap resamples of scenarios or episodes. The seeds are fixed in each preregistration.
\item Each preregistration (the document and its analysis code) is locked by its SHA-256 hash before its data exist. One script checks every lock and every number in this paper again from the saved results.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Compute.} All GPU work runs on NVIDIA H100 NVL GPUs (96~GB), one job at a time except for one 8-hour period with two (and one accidental 4-second overlap): PyTorch 2.11, Transformers 5.14.1 (5.20.1 for Muse-Glimmer, whose architecture the earlier version does not know), scikit-learn 1.9.0. The reader takes 41~ms for one action on one GPU; the median server round trip in the three blocking live tests was 89, 80 and 83~ms.

\subsection{The geometry experiments (G1--G5)}
\label{app:gdetails}
\textbf{Contexts and battery.} 4{,}800 single-message contexts, 800 from each of six sources (harmful and benign requests, SST-2 reviews, BoolQ questions, GSM8K problems, Wikipedia text), split at random into 3{,}360 development and 1{,}440 confirmation contexts before any analysis. The battery has 88 yes/no questions about the message (topic, intent, tone, demands, risk), each appended after a fixed preamble that asks for a one-word answer; $P(\textsc{yes})=p_{\textsc{yes}}/(p_{\textsc{yes}}+p_{\textsc{no}})$ from the top-20 log-probabilities of the first answer token (cells where both are $0$, 2 of 38{,}400 per model, get $\tfrac12$).
\textbf{Models.} Gemma-3 4B, gpt-oss 20B, Llama-3.1 8B, Mistral 7B v0.3, OLMo-3 7B, Phi-4 mini, Qwen2.5 7B and Qwen3 8B, instruction-tuned, with their own chat templates (vLLM for answers and replies, Hugging Face transformers for hidden states). Hidden states: the residual stream at the last prompt token at six depths (25, 50, 62, 75, 90 and 100\% of the layers). The layer of each model is the one whose cosine geometry best matches the exact answer geometry on 1{,}000 development contexts; the confirmation contexts are not used to choose anything.
\textbf{Sampling.} Sampled answers are drawn from the model's own probabilities (8 per question for G1; one per question, as yes, no or other, for G5). Text and reply embeddings: all-MiniLM-L6-v2, masked mean, $L_2$ normalised; replies are 8 samples at temperature 1, at most 128 tokens.
\textbf{Closed-A (G2).} Closed-A answered the 24 questions of our earlier closed-model tests, 8 times each, on 192 confirmation contexts (thinking off, at most 3 tokens). The two halves are a random permutation of each cell's 8 samples (seed 2290). The bound of C1 (0.7) was set from open models' simulated split halves on development contexts only (0.76--0.96).
\textbf{Exact recovery (G3).} Qwen3-0.6B in float32 (TF32 off). 9{,}000 sequences of 128 tokens sampled from the model (temperature 1; seed 0), every position a context. The basis $Q_d$ is from the first 3{,}000 contexts ($\sigma_d/\sigma_{d+1}=1{,}005$). The true state for the check is the input of the final RMSNorm divided by its RMS. A pilot with another seed (100) gave the same result to the fourth digit.
\textbf{Bootstrap.} 500 resamples of contexts; Spearman on the pairs of distinct resampled contexts.
