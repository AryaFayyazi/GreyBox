\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Interpretability reads what a model knows and what it will do from its hidden state \citep{burns2023ccs,marks2023geometry,zou2023repe,arditi2024refusal,goldowskydill2025probes}. For agents, a probe on the hidden state can stop an unsafe action before it runs \citep{fayyazi2026cape}. These tools need the weights. The most capable models are closed: their providers return sampled text and, at most, some log-probabilities.

\textbf{Why read the state, not the text.} Agents make calls that they are not authorized to make, and what they say about it can mislead: in CAPE, four open frontier agents that knew a call was unauthorized made it and then denied it \citep{fayyazi2026cape}, and chain-of-thought often leaves out what drives an answer \citep{turpin2023unfaithful,chen2025reasoning}. We find the same gap in a closed agent. Before its call, Claude Haiku 5.5's answers about its next action do not forecast its coming violation (0.47 among calls at the same step), but its own sampled next actions, read through its API, do (0.71 on the same episodes), and on a second set the same model reasoning over the full transcript scores 0.45 where its sampled actions score 0.89. At the call, asked whether its unapproved restarts complied, it mostly said yes (AUROC 0.32), while its answers to eight specific questions ranked them at 0.91. So we read the state behind the text.

\textbf{What can an API reveal?} Not hidden coordinates: any change of basis that the next layers undo leaves every output unchanged (\cref{prop:gauge}). Geometry survives. We ask the model yes/no questions in \emph{side branches} (\cref{fig:method}): copies of the conversation, each with one question appended, sent through its API and then discarded, so the agent never sees them. In the angles $\vartheta=2\arcsin\sqrt{P(\textsc{yes})}$, the Fisher--Rao distance between answer laws is Euclidean (\cref{thm:geometry}), and its noise is known. Nothing is trained.

\textbf{What we find} (\cref{fig:teaser}). \emph{(i) What an API reveals} (\cref{sec:geom-results}): on held-out contexts of eight open models, the answer geometry tracks each model's hidden geometry better than the input or the replies do, and it is shared across models (own-model excess +0.002); Claude Haiku 5.5's answers agree with open models' hidden geometry at 0.45--0.51. \emph{(ii) At the call, any capable reader suffices} (\cref{sec:monitor,sec:carry}): the transcript and the call decide a violation (\cref{cor:action}), so a fixed read-out of the agent's answers, never fit to labels, ranks violations nearly as well as a probe on its hidden states, carries to two external benchmarks and two closed agents, and another model's answers do as well or better. \emph{(iii) Before the call, the forecast is the agent's policy} (\cref{prop:selfforecast,sec:forecast}), and text suffices only when the agent's words track its actions. For GPT-6-luna they do: its self-report and a reasoning text monitor forecast its coming violation. For Claude Haiku 5.5 they do not: both are at or below chance, and a stronger text monitor from another provider does little better, while its own sampled next actions forecast the violation (\cref{cor:rollout}). A label-free check tells the two cases apart (exploratory): the agreement between what an agent says about its next action and what its sampled policy does. \emph{(iv) Prevention} (\cref{sec:prevent-live}): with no human in the loop, the agents' own read-outs, holding a call once for it to be re-checked, cut violating episodes from 67\% to 35\% (Claude Haiku 5.5) and from 27\% to 17.5\% (GPT-6-luna), more than random holds.

\textbf{Contributions.} Theory of what an API reveals and of monitoring versus forecasting (\cref{sec:method}; classical parts marked), evidence on eight open models, four open agents, two closed agents and three benchmarks, and a released tool (\cref{app:tool}).

\textbf{Preregistration.} We hash-locked every confirmatory test before its data existed (or, for a test on existing data, before its analysis), and published the later locks before the run. A result is preregistered unless marked exploratory. Of the method's tests, many passed and some failed; \cref{app:prereg} lists every test with its outcome, and each claim below names the failed tests that bear on it.
