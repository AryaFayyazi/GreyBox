\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Interpretability reads what a model knows and what it will do from its hidden state \citep{burns2023ccs,marks2023geometry,zou2023repe,arditi2024refusal,goldowskydill2025probes}. For agents, a probe on the hidden state can stop an unsafe action before it runs \citep{fayyazi2026cape}. These tools need the weights. The most capable models are closed: their providers return sampled text and, at most, some log-probabilities.

\textbf{Why ask the agent, not only read its text.} Agents make calls that they are not authorized to make, and what they say about it can mislead: in CAPE, four open frontier agents that knew a call was unauthorized made it and then denied it \citep{fayyazi2026cape}, and chain-of-thought often leaves out what drives an answer \citep{turpin2023unfaithful,chen2025reasoning}. We find the same gap in a closed agent, Claude Haiku 5.5. Before a call, its answers about its next action do not forecast the coming violation. Neither does the same model reasoning over the full transcript. Its own next actions, sampled through its API, do. The next action follows from the agent's state, and the text does not show that state. But the API lets us sample the agent's policy, so we read what the agent does, not only what it says.

\textbf{What can an API reveal?} Not hidden coordinates: any change of basis that the next layers undo leaves every output unchanged (\cref{prop:gauge}). Geometry survives. We ask the model yes/no questions in \emph{side branches} (\cref{fig:method}): copies of the conversation, each with one question appended, sent through its API and then discarded, so the agent never sees them. In the angles $\vartheta=2\arcsin\sqrt{P(\textsc{yes})}$, the Fisher--Rao distance between answer laws is Euclidean (\cref{thm:geometry}), and its noise is known. Nothing is trained.

\textbf{What we find} (\cref{fig:teaser}).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{An API reveals a shared geometry} (\cref{sec:geom-results}). On held-out contexts of eight open models, the answer geometry tracks each model's hidden geometry better than sentence embeddings of the input or the replies (on 6 or 7 of 8 models, against the strongest of five embedders). The geometry is also shared: a model's answers match another model's hidden geometry as well as its own (mean own-model excess +0.002).
\item \textbf{At the call, any capable reader suffices} (\cref{sec:monitor,sec:carry}). The transcript and the call decide a violation (\cref{cor:action}). A fixed read-out of the agent's answers, never fit to labels, ranks violations nearly as well as a probe on hidden states (0.82--0.99 on four open agents, 0.98 on GPT-6-luna), and another model's answers do as well.
\item \textbf{Before the call, the forecast is the agent's policy} (\cref{prop:selfforecast,sec:forecast}), which the API supplies by sampling. When a checker labels the sampled actions, the theory also predicts how much worse a forecaster that does not sample will be. On Claude Haiku 5.5's one task family with varying violations, among calls at the same step, its self-report (0.47) and two text monitors that reason over the transcript (at most 0.60) are near chance or weak. Its own sampled next actions forecast which call violates (0.89). Another model given the same context does not (0.54). These results replicated on 492 fresh episodes. GPT-6-luna's words, by contrast, track its actions (self-report 0.84), a reading we made after the data.
\item \textbf{The agent can police itself} (\cref{sec:prevent-live}). With no human in the loop, holding a call that the agent's own read-out flags cut Claude Haiku 5.5's violating episodes from 68\% to 35\%, against 43\% for random holds at the same dose; a plain policy reminder did better still (18\%).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Contributions.} Theory of what an API reveals and of monitoring versus forecasting (\cref{sec:method}; mostly elementary or classical, marked; its value is in predictions fixed before the data), evidence on eight open models, four open agents, two closed agents and three benchmarks, and a released tool (\cref{app:tool}).

\textbf{Preregistration.} We hash-locked every confirmatory test before its data existed (or, for a test on existing data, before its analysis) and published each lock before the run; a result is preregistered unless marked exploratory. Of the method's 74 tests, 35 passed every primary endpoint, 22 passed some and 13 failed; \cref{app:prereg} lists every test with its outcome, and each claim below names the failed tests that bear on it. Analyses registered on existing data after related results were known are marked secondary; their positive Claude Haiku 5.5 results were retested on fresh episodes and replicated (their GPT-6-luna parts were not retested). Three headline tests redesign earlier tests that failed (the prevention law, the pre-call gate, Claude's forecast with exact contexts), reported next to them. Over all 37 registered endpoints of the closed-agent tests behind the abstract, 25 survive a Holm correction (exploratory).
