\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Interpretability reads what a model knows and what it will do from its hidden state \citep{burns2023ccs,marks2023geometry,zou2023repe,arditi2024refusal,goldowskydill2025probes}. For agents, a probe on the hidden state can stop an unsafe action before it runs \citep{fayyazi2026cape}. These tools need the weights. The most capable models are closed: their providers return sampled text and, at most, some log-probabilities.

\textbf{Why read the state, not the text.} Agents make calls that they are not authorized to make, and when asked, they may say that the call is fine: agents that knew a call was unauthorized made it and then denied it \citep{fayyazi2026cape}. Chain-of-thought often leaves out what drives an answer \citep{turpin2023unfaithful,chen2025reasoning}. A rule check that the operator writes catches only the rules that it names, and it needs facts spread through the transcript: the same \texttt{restart} is allowed after an approval three messages earlier and forbidden without one. So we read the state behind the text. In our data the gap is plain. Asked directly whether its unapproved production restarts complied, Claude Haiku 5.5 mostly said yes (AUROC 0.32, below chance). Its answers to eight specific questions about the same calls ranked them at 0.91.

\textbf{Geometry, not coordinates.} An API cannot reveal a model's hidden coordinates: any change of basis that the next layers undo leaves every output unchanged (\cref{prop:gauge}). What survives is geometry. We ask the model yes/no questions in \emph{side branches}: copies of the conversation so far, each with one question appended, sent through the model's API and then discarded. The agent never sees them. In the angles $\vartheta=2\arcsin\sqrt{P(\textsc{yes})}$, the Fisher--Rao distance between answer laws is plain Euclidean distance (\cref{thm:geometry}). This is a change of variables: it needs no training, and sampled text from any chat API estimates it. At a proposed call, the agent's answers to a few such questions are its \emph{observable state} at the decision. A fixed read-out of them, with no labels, monitors the call before it runs.

\textbf{What we find.} The results form one chain, and each step uses the one before it.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \emph{Reconstruct.} On eight open models, the geometry of a model's answers tracks its hidden geometry better than the input text or its replies do. Much of this geometry is shared across models. Through the shared part, a triangulation estimates that Claude Haiku 5.5's answers agree with its own unseen hidden geometry at 0.49 (\cref{sec:geom-results}).
\item \emph{Monitor.} At a proposed call, a fixed read-out of the agent's answers ranks its violations nearly as well as a probe trained on its hidden states, and better than a reasoning judge on open agents. With no label, the read-out's own noise tells where it works. It carries to prompt injection (0.90--0.99) and to two closed agents: GPT-6-luna (0.98) and Claude Haiku 5.5 (0.91 on unapproved restarts, 0.70 on airline writes; \cref{sec:monitor,sec:carry}).
\item \emph{Forecast.} Before the call exists, GPT-6-luna's answers about its next action rank the coming violation at 0.92 among calls at the same position, on fresh episodes. Its own answers forecast better than another model's answers in its seat (0.84 vs 0.73). The theory predicts this: at the call, any capable reader can match the agent, but before the call only the agent's own policy gives the forecast (\cref{prop:selfforecast,sec:forecast}).
\item \emph{Prevent.} The agent's own answers act as a live gate. The gate prevented 82\% of an open agent's violating episodes and 69\% of those that Claude Haiku 5.5's transcript decides, and offline flag rates predicted the prevention of five agents. On GPT-6-luna, a gate on the pre-call state cut violating episodes from 32\% to 8\%, against 18\% for a gate on position at the same review rate (\cref{sec:prevent-live}).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) \emph{Theory} (\cref{sec:method}): what an API reveals of a hidden state (geometry, not coordinates); exact coordinates and an unbiased estimator from sampled answers; a triangulation that checks a closed model's reconstruction; a label-free ceiling on the read-out; a proof that the forecast needs the agent's own policy; and a state-space model that unlabelled runs identify. (2) \emph{Evidence} on eight open models, four open agents, two closed agents and three benchmarks (\cref{sec:geom-results,sec:results}). (3) A released tool (\cref{sec:tool}).

\textbf{Preregistration.} We preregistered every confirmatory test: we hash-locked its document and code before the data existed, and for later tests we published the lock first. A result is preregistered unless we mark it exploratory. The record (\cref{app:record}) lists every test, the failed tests included, and \cref{app:limits} gives the limits of each claim.
