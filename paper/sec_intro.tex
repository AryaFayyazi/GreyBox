\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Interpretability reads what a model knows and what it will do from its hidden state \citep{burns2023ccs,marks2023geometry,zou2023repe,arditi2024refusal,goldowskydill2025probes}. For agents, a probe on the hidden state can stop an unsafe action before it runs \citep{fayyazi2026cape}. These tools need the weights. The most capable models are closed: their providers return sampled text and, at most, some log-probabilities.

\textbf{Why an outside read of the state.} Agents make calls that they are not authorized to make, and their words cannot be trusted to say so: in CAPE, four open frontier agents that knew a call was unauthorized made it and then denied it \citep{fayyazi2026cape}, and chain-of-thought often leaves out what drives an answer \citep{turpin2023unfaithful,chen2025reasoning}. A monitor needs the agent's state, read from outside its own account. For a closed agent the only outside access is its API.

\textbf{What can an API reveal of the hidden state?} Not hidden coordinates: any change of basis that the next layers undo leaves every output unchanged (\cref{prop:gauge}). Geometry survives. We ask the model yes/no questions in \emph{side branches} (\cref{fig:method}): copies of the conversation with one question appended, discarded after the answer, so the agent never sees them. In the angles $\vartheta=2\arcsin\sqrt{P(\textsc{yes})}$, the Fisher--Rao distance between answer laws is Euclidean, its noise is known, and near a state it is the hidden geometry seen through the metric that the questions pull back (\cref{thm:geometry}). Nothing is trained.

\textbf{What we find} (\cref{fig:teaser}). The theory has three steps, and each makes a prediction that we test.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The geometry is estimable through the API} (\cref{sec:geom-results}). On eight open models, the answer geometry tracks the true hidden geometry (0.41--0.74), better than embeddings of the input or of the model's replies. Sampled answers reveal the part of the geometry that models share; the model-private part appears only in log-probabilities. Through Claude Haiku 5.5's API, on fresh contexts, its answers track that shared geometry better than every text baseline on 7 of 8 models (a first test with 24 of the 88 questions failed), though, like open models' own answers, not clearly on new kinds. At an agent's decision, 8 questions read a slice of the state, not all of it.
\item \textbf{The geometry forecasts violations} (\cref{sec:monitor,sec:forecast}). At the call, the transcript carries most of the signal, so any capable answerer does about as well as the agent (\cref{cor:action}). Before the call, the forecast is the agent's own policy (\cref{prop:selfforecast}): we sample its next actions and read them in the estimated geometry. Their distance from the agent's typical state, with no label and no question sign, forecasts Claude's violations (0.89; 0.87 on fresh episodes) and, on average, four open agents' (0.75). Claude's own words do not: before 93\% of its violating calls, none of its 32 answers admitted a violation (65\% before safe calls). So the sampling supplies the signal and the geometry reads it with no label or task knowledge: within Claude's one family simple rules do as well, but across open agents' families, where a generic rule is at chance, it nearly matches a label-using checker (0.75 vs 0.79; exploratory). A white-box probe reading the agent's private reasoning forecasts better (by 0.16); at the turn's start the signed API forecast matched a probe.
\item \textbf{The geometry prevents violations} (\cref{sec:prevent-live}). Live, with no human in the loop, a gate on the label-free distance beat random holds at the same rate on GPT-6-luna across six task families (10\% vs 15\% of episodes violating) and on Claude's restarts (20\% vs 32\%, a redesign); on new restart worlds one hold was not enough (22\% vs 25\%), but holding until the distance cleared was (7.7\% vs 26.2\%; task success unchanged).
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Contributions.} The first training-free estimate of hidden-state geometry from a closed API's sampled answers, with exactly known noise, checked against true hidden states (prior work needs log-probabilities or weights, or never makes this check; its parts are classical, \cref{sec:related}); its use to forecast and prevent violations of closed agents; evidence on 8 open models, 6 agents, 3 benchmarks; a tool (\cref{app:tool}).

\textbf{Preregistration.} We hash-locked every confirmatory test before its data existed (or, for a test on existing data, before its analysis) and published each lock before the run; a result is preregistered unless marked exploratory. Of the method's 87 tests, 41 passed every primary endpoint, 29 passed some, 14 failed, 2 stopped early and 1 is pending; \cref{app:prereg} lists every test with its outcome, and each claim below names the failed tests that bear on it. Analyses registered after related results were known are marked secondary; Claude's positive ones replicated on fresh episodes (GPT-6-luna's were not retested). Five headline tests redesign failed ones (listed in \cref{app:limits}); each was locked before its new data and is reported next to the failure. Of 64 closed-agent endpoints, 48 survive Holm (exploratory).
