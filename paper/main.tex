\begin{abstract}
Probes on an agent's hidden states can stop an unsafe action before it runs, but they need the weights, and the most capable agents are closed. We ask what a closed agent's own API reveals of its state, with no training and no other model, and when the agent itself is needed to monitor it. An API cannot reveal hidden coordinates (a gauge symmetry), but it reveals geometry: yes/no questions asked in throw-away copies of the conversation give answer laws whose Fisher--Rao distances are exact Euclidean distances in arcsine coordinates, with a known bound on its noise. On eight open models this geometry tracks each model's hidden geometry, and it is shared across models; on two closed agents, a call's distance from the agent's typical state in this geometry flags its violations with no label (AUROC 0.82), and the same distance of its sampled next actions forecasts them before the call. Our theory then separates two regimes, and preregistered experiments on two closed agents confirm both. \emph{At a proposed call}, the transcript decides the violation, so any capable reader suffices: a fixed read-out of the agent's answers ranks violations at AUROC 0.82--0.99 on four open agents and 0.98 on GPT-6-luna with no labels, and other models do as well or better. \emph{Before the call}, the forecast is the agent's policy, and text suffices only when the agent's words track its actions. GPT-6-luna's do (its self-report 0.84; a reasoning text monitor 0.78). Claude Haiku 5.5's do not: its self-report (0.47) and the same model reasoning over its transcript (0.45) are at or below chance, a stronger text monitor from another provider reaches only 0.60, while its own sampled next actions, on the same episodes, forecast the violation (0.71 and 0.89). \emph{Live}, with no human in the loop, the agents' own read-outs, holding a call once for it to be re-checked, cut violating episodes from 67\% to 35\% (Claude Haiku 5.5) and from 27\% to 17.5\% (GPT-6-luna), more than random holds. We report every preregistered test, passed or failed, and release the tool.
\end{abstract}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Information geometry and shared representations.} The Fisher metric, its uniqueness, the arcsine transform and classical scaling are classical \citep{cencov1982statistical,campbell1986extended,amari2016information,anscombe1948transformation,torgerson1952mds}; distance matrices are compared without a fit in representational similarity analysis \citep{kriegeskorte2008rsa,walther2016reliability}. Representations of different models converge \citep{huh2024platonic}; the Fisher--Rao geometry of next-token laws is shared across architectures \citep{picozzi2026information} \citep[see also][]{schiekiera2026associations}.

\textbf{Black-box access.} A production model's output projection is recovered up to an orthogonal map \citep{finlayson2024logits,carlini2024stealing}; \cref{thm:exact} uses this structure for the hidden geometry. Answers to questions serve as embeddings \citep{benara2024crafting}, detect lies \citep{pacchiardi2024liar,cooney2026didyoulie,sam2025quere} and forecast behaviour \citep{levy2026forecasting}; open models monitor closed agents but need a second model \citep{conti2026hermeneutics,zhao2026proxy}. Self-prediction beats other models only after fine-tuning \citep{binder2025looking}, and hidden-state probes beat an agent's verbal predictions of its tool calls \citep{advani2026reading}; through the API alone we find the same gap in a closed agent's self-report, and read its policy by sampling instead.

\textbf{Probes and monitors.} Probes on internals read truth, refusal and deception \citep{burns2023ccs,marks2023geometry,zou2023repe,arditi2024refusal,goldowskydill2025probes}, and CAPE probes an agent before each action \citep{fayyazi2026cape}. Chain-of-thought monitors catch reward hacking that action-only monitors miss \citep{baker2025monitoring,korbak2025cot,chen2025reasoning,arnav2025redhanded}. Open models' hidden states serve as features for a closed model \citep{sriramanan2024llmcheck}, and white-box probes join black-box monitors where activations exist \citep{parrack2025blacktowhite}; we read a closed agent through its own answers, with no training; yes/no checklists also make LLM judges agree better with people \citep{cook2024tick,lee2024checkeval}.

\textbf{Self-policing and control.} Agents recognize their own misconduct after the fact \citep{li2025odcv}; checks of the world before a write recover silent violations \citep{reddy2026reasonless}; resampling limits an adversarial agent \citep{bhatt2025ctrlz}; AI control audits a fixed budget of actions \citep{greenblatt2024control}, and our frozen cut is split conformal prediction \citep{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023conformal}. Self-policing is untrusted monitoring without an adversary; it cannot stop a model that colludes with itself.

\section{Conclusion}
A closed agent's API reveals the geometry of its state, shared across models. At the call, distance in this geometry flags violations with no label, and a fixed read-out of the agent's answers monitors them. Before the call, the forecast is the agent's policy: text suffices when its words track its actions; when they do not, as for Claude Haiku 5.5, its sampled actions forecast what text misses. Through the API alone, these reads prevented violations live on two closed agents.

\section*{Impact Statement}
This work monitors closed AI agents before they act, through their own APIs and with no training. The intended effect is safer deployment: in our tests, a gate on the agent's own answers prevented 82\% of an open agent's violating episodes and 69\% of those that a closed agent's transcript decides. We see four risks. (1) False confidence: the read-out cannot see a violation decided by a fact the agent never checked, and its questions cover the kinds of violation they were written for; a new policy needs new questions. (2) Holding calls costs time, and a held allowed call can end an episode early (GPT-6-luna's success changed by $-$0.016 [$-$0.056, 0.028]). (3) An agent that knows the questions, or an attacker who queries a score, may learn to answer them falsely; the questions and scores should stay hidden from the agent, and self-policing is no defense against a model that colludes with itself. (4) Reading a model's state from outside could help an attacker study a closed model; the method uses only ordinary answers and recovers geometry, not weights or private data. The code is released under the MIT license, and the browser tool does not upload the user's logs.

\section{The released tool}
\label{app:tool}
The website's browser trainer imports agent logs, lists the riskiest unlabelled action first for review, and trains a reader monitor in the browser, so the logs never leave the user's machine; the monitor then runs in a tool loop, an MCP proxy or an agent framework.

\section{Smaller readers}
\label{app:small}
This appendix tests readers smaller than the shipped one, including encoders that run in the browser. All readers are trained on the same 17{,}623 Gemma-4 CAPE-Bench steps (1{,}339 violations); none is trained on a test set. Browser encoders: all-MiniLM-L6-v2, all-mpnet-base-v2 (three 1{,}000-character views) and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (one instruction-prefixed text, last-token pooling), each with a standardised logistic probe. Decoder readers: Qwen3-0.6B, 1.7B, 4B and 14B at the shipped reader's relative depth, with the shipped probe. The selected browser encoder, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, beats TF-IDF on the closed agent's AgentDojo calls (0.605 vs 0.335; $+0.270$ [0.134, 0.451]), so the website offers it for new kinds of tasks. In the browser it runs as a float16 ONNX model whose features match PyTorch's (cosine 1.000 on 40 steps); the 8-bit model's do not (0.852).

\section{The observable state in chat}
\label{app:psi}
On Claude Haiku 5.5, readouts of the observable state trained only on open models forecast which harmful requests Claude Haiku 5.5 refuses (\cref{fig:psi}). Against a three-level moderation verdict the forecast wins (three preregistered tests). Combined with a moderator, it doubles the precision of an alarm for harmful requests that the model answers (0.535 vs 0.274).
