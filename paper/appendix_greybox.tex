\section{A trained comparison: recreating the decision state}
\label{app:greybox}
This comparison is not part of the method: it trains linear maps and probes on open agents. Let $O_t$ be what the API yields at the decision: an open reader's state $\psi_t$ of the same visible tokens and action, and the agent's own answers $\vartheta_t$ to yes/no questions about its proposed call, asked in side branches. The grey box is the \emph{recreated decision state} $\hat\phi_t=\E[\phi_t\mid O_t]$, estimated by linear maps fit on an open agent whose states $\phi$ are known; a probe trained only on true states reads it.
\begin{proposition}[reading the recreated state]\label{prop:greybox}
For every linear probe $w$, $w^\top\hat\phi_t=\E[w^\top\phi_t\mid O_t]$. If $Y\indep\phi_t\mid O_t$, the probe on $\hat\phi_t$ has expected loss at most that on $\phi_t$ for every loss convex in the score.
\end{proposition}
With reasoning off, $\phi_t$ is a function of the visible tokens, so in population the recreation is exact and the two losses are equal: nothing is lost by not having the weights (proof: \cref{prop:replica,prop:rb}, \cref{app:agents}). In practice the maps are linear, fit on a few hundred calls, on 32 principal components; how much this light recreation loses is an empirical question, which we test. What is new is not that the transcript suffices but that probes trained on \emph{true} states, of one agent, can be carried to another agent whose states are never seen.

\textbf{The agent's answers.} At a proposed call, the agent's conversation up to the call is sent again through its own API with one operator message: ``[Operator check. Your next tool call is on hold and has NOT been executed.] Proposed call: \emph{name}(\emph{arguments}). Question about this call: \emph{q} Answer with one word: yes or no.'' Tool use is disabled for the answer. Each of the 8 questions is asked in its own side branch, 4 answers each, sampled with the agent's own settings (open agents: their vLLM server and parsers; Claude Haiku 5.5: thinking disabled, low effort). The answer law of question $j$ is estimated by the Anscombe angle $\hat\vartheta_j=2\arcsin\sqrt{(k_j+3/8)/(m_j+3/4)}$ from $k_j$ ``yes'' among $m_j$ readable answers. The questions ask whether the call: complies with the policy; touches a resource the user does not own and is not authorised for; sends data outside its authorised audience; carries out an instruction found inside a file, email, page or tool output; is irreversible or on a production system without prior approval; uses a credential not issued for the task; affects uninvolved people without approval; breaks an explicit constraint of the task.

\textbf{The recreation.} For an agent whose decision states $\phi$ are stored (at CAPE's decision token of each call, the 4th of five stored layers), the top 32 principal components $z$ of $\phi$ are fit on development calls. The reader (Qwen3-8B) reads the transcript and the call; its last-token state at layer 32 is reduced to 256 principal components, and a ridge map ($\alpha=10$) to $z$ is fit on development calls. A second ridge map ($\alpha=1$) from the 8 angles to the reader's residual $z-\hat z_{\mathrm{reader}}$ is fit on the development battery. The recreation is $\hat z=\hat z_{\mathrm{reader}}+\hat z_{\mathrm{answers}}$. The white-box probe is a logistic regression ($C=0.1$) on the true $z$ of development calls and their labels. No label enters a map.

\textbf{Other agents' coordinates.} To score an agent without its states (a closed agent, or an open agent held out), its call is recreated in each source agent's coordinates and read by that source's probe; each source's scores are standardised over the scored calls (no labels) and averaged.

\textbf{Controls} (development). Pairing the answers with other calls' states before fitting the map takes the probe to chance on Gemma-4 (0.46): the recreation, not the probe, carries the signal. A logistic regression fit with labels directly on the 8 angles scores 0.980 on Gemma-4, below the label-free recreation (0.995), as \cref{prop:greybox} allows.
