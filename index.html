Monitor AI agents you can't open.

Grey Box decides whether an AI agent's next action should go to a human before it runs, reading only what every deployment can see: the agent's instructions, the user's task, earlier tool results and the proposed action. It needs no access to the model's weights, so it works on closed models. Every claim here comes from a preregistered, hash-locked test, failures included.

What we found

Labels, not access

A small open model that reads the transcript, trained on labelled examples, matches the agent's own internal activations (AUROC 0.994 vs 0.979). Trained only on an open agent, it caught 96% of a closed frontier agent's violations (Claude Haiku 5.5) with 5% of actions sent to review. A zero-shot 70B judge caught 0%.

Time, not access

White-box probes look better than transcript monitors mainly because they read later: after the model's hidden reasoning, the action is nearly decided (probe 0.979 after reasoning vs 0.841 before it). A monitor that sees the proposed action gets the same advantage.

The policy is the limit, not the agent

For a policy family the monitor never saw, it still ranked Claude's violations above safe actions (AUROC 0.912 vs judge 0.570), but escalated none of them at a 5% budget. Adding 20 labelled steps of that policy brought it to 85%, and 40 to 94%. Label a few dozen steps of each policy you enforce.

Simple monitors suffice, until the task changes

A word-count classifier trained on the same labels matches the reader in distribution (Claude: 0.997 vs 0.991). On a new benchmark of prompt injections it fell to chance (0.50), where the reader held 0.78.

Set the review threshold on your own traffic

A monitor's probabilities do not transfer between agents (a 0.5 cut caught 3% on Claude); its ranking does. Escalating the top 5% of the agent's own first 500 actions, then freezing that cut, caught 95% online and held at 94% when half of a later burst was malicious. A rolling cut fell to 36%.

Chat: forecasting refusals

Asking a model about a conversation in a side branch forecasts whether it will refuse. On Claude it ties a strong graded moderator at ranking refusals, and combined with one it doubles the precision of a "harmful request answered" alarm. It is not a better content filter.

Limits. Most agent tests use one benchmark (CAPE-Bench) and one closed agent family so far (a second, GPT, is under a locked test); the external test (AgentDojo) is weaker (AUROC 0.78). Monitors must be trained on labelled steps of the policies they enforce. The chat readouts recover what behaviour reveals, not a model's hidden beliefs. See the full evidence.
