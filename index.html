Monitor AI agents that you cannot open.

Grey Box decides if an AI agent's next action must go to a human before the action runs. It reads only the data that every deployment can see: the agent's instructions, the user's task, the earlier tool results and the proposed action. It does not need the model's weights, so it works on closed models. Each claim on this site comes from a preregistered, hash-locked test. We show the failures too.

What we found

Labels, not access

A small open model reads the transcript. We train it on labelled examples. It is equal to or better than the agent's own internal activations (AUROC 0.994 vs 0.979). We trained it only on an open agent. On two closed agents, on its training benchmark, with 5% of the actions sent to review, it caught 96% (Claude Haiku 5.5) and 98% (GPT-6-luna) of the violations. A zero-shot 70B judge caught 0% and 38%.

Why access does not help: a theorem

An agent's internal state is a function of the tokens that it read. For the label "does this action break the policy?", a monitor that reads the transcript and the proposed action is Bayes-sufficient. Internals read at or before the action cannot do better. White-box probes win against turn-start monitors only because they read later (probe 0.979 after the reasoning, 0.841 before it).

The limit is the policy, not the agent

We removed a policy family from the training data. A credentials policy with similar trained policies still transferred: the monitor caught all of GPT's violations of it with no labels. A service-restart policy without similar ones did not: the monitor ranked Claude's violations well (AUROC 0.912) but escalated none at a 5% budget. With 20 labelled steps it caught 85%; with 40, 94%. Labels from open agents work for closed ones: on prompt injections, labelled calls of open agents raised the catch rate on a closed agent from 36% to 92%. Label steps of each new type of policy.

Simple monitors are enough, until the task changes

A word-count classifier with the same labels is equal to the reader on the training benchmark (Claude: 0.997 vs 0.991). On a new benchmark of prompt injections it fell to chance (0.50). The reader stayed at 0.78.

Set the review threshold from your own traffic

A monitor's probabilities do not transfer between agents: a 0.5 cut caught 3% on Claude. Its ranking transfers. We escalate the top 5% of the agent's first 500 actions and then freeze that threshold. It caught 95% online. When half of a later burst was malicious, it still caught 94%. A rolling threshold caught 36%.

Chat: forecast a refusal

We ask a model about a conversation in a side branch. The answers forecast if the model will refuse. On Claude this forecast only ties a strong graded moderator. Together with a moderator, it doubles the precision of an alarm for harmful requests that the model answers. It is not a better content filter.

Limits. Most agent tests use one benchmark (CAPE-Bench). Two closed agent families are tested (Claude, GPT). On an external benchmark of prompt injections, the reader without labels of that policy was not significantly better than a zero-shot judge on a closed agent (AUROC 0.840 vs 0.787); with labelled calls from open agents it reached 0.922. A monitor needs labelled steps of each type of policy that it enforces. The chat readouts recover a model's behaviour, not its hidden beliefs. See all the evidence.
