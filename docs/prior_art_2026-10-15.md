## A. Behavioural sufficiency: "probes cannot beat rollouts"

### The formal point (and why a reviewer will call it near-trivial)

Suppose the label Y is a function of the model's own continuation C, with C sampled from p_θ(·|x) at a fixed decoding policy. The rollout forecaster π(x) = P(Y=1|x) is then the Bayes-optimal forecast given the prefix. Any hidden-state feature h = g(x) satisfies E[Y|h] = E[π(x)|h], by the tower property. So no probe on h can beat π under a proper scoring rule.

Transformer LMs are empirically and provably injective (2510.15511), so h determines x. The Bayes-optimal probe therefore *equals* π, not just is dominated by it. The result follows from conditioning in a few lines, and I found **no paper that states it as a theorem for safety monitoring**.

Close relatives in the literature:
- **Twisted SMC.** Learned "twists" in 2404.17546 *are* approximations of this conditional expectation.
- **Supervision targets.** StreamGuard (2604.03962) and Lugoloobi et al. (2602.09924) use MC rollout averages as the target for a prefix-level predictor. This implicitly treats π as ground truth.

The real content therefore has to come from three areas:
1. **Finite-sample behaviour**:
   - how many rollouts a probe is "worth";
   - amortisation;
   - rare events, where MC needs ~1/p samples.
2. **Labels that are *not* functions of the model's own continuation from that prefix**:
   - "lie" defined against the model's beliefs, which is behaviour under *other* prompts;
   - environment-stochastic agent outcomes;
   - changes in decoding policy.
3. **Distribution shift and adversarial robustness** of probes versus rollouts.

### Verdict for A

- **Already done.** Several papers use probes to amortise sampling-defined quantities: semantic entropy, outcome distributions, numeric predictive distributions, rollout-supervised harm values. Some papers estimate safety risk by MC continuation sampling (ProbGuard 2608.10621, StreamGuard targets). Belief-state theory says hidden states encode the predictive distribution over futures.
- **Not found.**
  1. An explicit sufficiency statement or theorem framed for safety monitoring ("for behavioural labels, rollouts ≥ probes in the infinite-sample limit, with equality by injectivity").
  2. A matched-compute head-to-head on *safety/agent* labels: white-box probe vs N-rollout MC forecaster vs black-box suffix-query forecaster, with "probe ≈ N rollouts" curves.
  3. A clean taxonomy of labels where white-box *can* beat rollouts: belief-relative deception, labels that depend on counterfactual prompts, environment stochasticity, rare events.
- **Reviewer risk.** "The theorem is the tower property, and injectivity makes it an equality." Frame it as an organising principle, and make the empirical sample-efficiency and label-taxonomy study the contribution. The FIBS "introspective deception" and Cooney et al. model-organism results are natural counterexamples to cite, because their labels are not functions of the continuation.

### Verdict for B

- **Already done.** Using answers or logprobs to appended questions as a black-box representation is established (2309.15840, 2501.01558). QueRE already reports beating white-box linear probes on correctness. Cooney et al. (2606.12618) already ran the black-box suffix-query detector against activation probes on deception across 31 models and found it collapses on belief-verified model organisms.
- **Neighbouring uses already exist**:
  - harmfulness detection (2512.16650);
  - reasoning-trajectory featurisation (2503.22165);
  - reward-hacking detection by forced answers (2510.01367);
  - forked persona batteries in long agent sessions (2605.24279).
- **Open.**
  1. Treating the battery explicitly as a **state estimator**: regress or CCA the true residual stream from query answers, report variance explained per layer, and analyse rank and conditioning.
  2. Using it on **agentic sabotage or policy-violation forecasting** (SHADE/BASH-Arena-style), compared with probes and rollouts.
  3. Principled battery design: PSR "core tests", Hankel rank, Fisher-information-optimal questions.
  4. Forking the agent at every step to get a state *trajectory*.
- **Reviewer will say.** "QueRE plus Pacchiardi plus Cooney already compared black-box follow-ups to probes." Novelty must come from points 1-4 above and from agent settings.

### Verdict for C

- **Already done.**
  - The final-layer state is linearly identifiable from full logits (softmax bottleneck), and API leakage has been exploited for model identity and hidden size.
  - Prompt inversion from logprobs exists.
  - Probe transfer exists in white-box-to-white-box form (stitching) and via relative anchors.
  - "Do open-model mechanisms transfer to closed models?" was studied, with cautionary results, in 2606.32008.
  - Fisher-Rao output geometry as model-agnostic coordinates appears in 2609.11063, from last month.
- **Not found.**
  1. Predicting **intermediate-layer residual streams** from black-box signals (suffix-query answers or sampled continuations), validated against ground-truth activations on open models.
  2. Mapping a white-box probe direction into the black-box query-feature space (probe-direction regression) and showing it transfers to closed models.
  3. Any modern PSR/Hankel/spectral treatment of LLMs or agents.
  4. Fisher-information-weighted query batteries as latent coordinates.
- **Reviewer will say.** For the final layer with full logits this is known (Finlayson; Nazir et al.). Real APIs give top-k or sampled outputs only. Demonstrate recovery under top-k-logprob or sample-only access, and of mid-layer states.

### Verdict for D

- **Largely done as of September-October 2026.**
  - Model Hermeneutics (LessWrong) measures the exact author-reader "faithfulness gap" on frontier closed models.
  - TRACES does observer-hidden-state agent risk forecasting.
  - Several October 2026 hallucination papers do observer probing.
  - SPAR has an active project on it.
- **Remaining room.**
  1. A rigorous control: does the reader probe beat a *text-only classifier fine-tuned on the same reader*? (cf. the 2608.00583 behavioural control and the 2511.17408 off-policy caveat.)
  2. Combining a reader's white-box features with the closed model's *own* black-box suffix-query answers (B), and showing complementarity.
  3. Theory linking the faithfulness gap to A: the reader can only recover what is inferable from the transcript, which is the transcript-sufficiency limit.

  Do not pitch D as novel on its own.

## Overall novelty summary

- **A.** Not stated as a theorem anywhere I found, but trivially provable. Several works implicitly treat rollouts as ground truth. Novel value lies in matched-compute empirics on safety and agent labels, and in a label taxonomy.
- **B.** Well established, including a white-box comparison (QueRE) and a deception head-to-head (Cooney et al. 2606.12618). Novel value lies in using it as an explicit state estimator validated against residual streams, in agents, and in principled battery design.
- **C.** Final-layer recovery from full logits is known. Mid-layer recovery from restricted black-box signals, probe-direction mapping into query space, and PSR/Hankel for LLMs all appear open.
- **D.** Essentially done in the last 6 weeks (Model Hermeneutics, TRACES, observer-probe papers, a SPAR project). Use it only as a baseline or component.
- **E.** Rollout safety forecasting exists for streaming text, and resampled-commitment labels exist. Agent-level black-box rollout forecasting vs probes vs suffix queries is open.
- **F.** Minor. Only 2D black-box precedent (LoT).

The most defensible combined contribution is a theory (A) explaining when black-box state estimates (B/C) and rollouts (E) can match white-box probes, plus where they provably cannot (non-behavioural labels). This would be backed by a three-way, matched-compute empirical study on agent sabotage and deception benchmarks, with validation of the recovered state against real residual streams on open models.
