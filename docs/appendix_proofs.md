# Appendix A: Proofs

Notation follows Section 2.
- B is a language model with a fixed decoding rule. x ∈ X is a context, and τ is the future generated from x by B
  together with an environment kernel E.
- Y = g(τ) ∈ {0, 1} is the label. φ: X → R^D is any internal representation.
- π*(x) = P(Y = 1 | x).
- Probabilities are over the joint law of (X, τ), where X is drawn from an evaluation distribution μ on contexts.

## A.1 Gauge (Proposition 1)

**Statement.** Let a model compute a residual stream h_ℓ ∈ R^d and read it only through maps of the form
h ↦ W h (attention projections, MLP input matrices, unembedding). Let it write to it only through additions of
vectors U v (embeddings, attention outputs, MLP outputs). Suppose there is no normalization, or normalization that is
invariant under the group considered (for example RMSNorm without gain under orthogonal maps). Then for every G in
that group, the model with W ↦ W G⁻¹ and U ↦ G U computes the same function, and its residual stream is G h_ℓ.

**Proof.** By induction on layers. The embedding writes G e instead of e. Suppose the rotated stream equals G h_ℓ
after layer ℓ. Each read sees W G⁻¹ G h_ℓ = W h_ℓ, the same input as before. Each component computes the same
output v and writes G U v. So the stream after layer ℓ + 1 is G h_{ℓ+1}. The unembedding reads W_U G⁻¹ G h_L =
W_U h_L, so the logits are identical. ∎

**Consequences.**
1. Every output-based statistic is invariant under G. So no black-box estimator can identify h up to anything finer
   than the group.
2. A probe w fitted on h gives w·G h ≠ w·h on the rotated model. White-box probes do not transfer across a change of
   basis without paired data.
3. In the tracr experiment, LayerNorm is absent, so the full orthogonal group applies.
For models with LayerNorm (which centres and rescales), the symmetry group is the orthogonal maps that fix the
all-ones direction, composed with the gain. The argument is unchanged. MLP neurons are not rotation-invariant: the
elementwise nonlinearity has a privileged basis. So per-neuron recovery is a well-defined target.

## A.2 Identifiability for finite-state behaviour (Proposition 2)

**Setting.** A black box computes f: Σ* → O, the output at the last position. Its behaviour is generated by a
deterministic finite automaton: there is a finite set Q, a start state q₀, a map δ: Q × Σ → Q and an output map
λ: Q → O, with f(x) = λ(δ*(q₀, x)). Let M be the minimal such automaton.

**Statement.**
(i) For prefixes x and x′: f(x s) = f(x′ s) for all s ∈ Σ* iff δ*_M(q₀, x) = δ*_M(q₀, x′).
(ii) A battery S ⊂ Σ* identifies the state of M iff for every pair of distinct reachable states p ≠ p′ some s ∈ S
     satisfies λ(δ*(p, s)) ≠ λ(δ*(p′, s)).
(iii) If M has n states, S = Σ^{≤ n−2} suffices.

**Proof.**
- (i) is the Myhill–Nerode theorem: the states of the minimal automaton are the classes of the right-congruence
  x ~ x′ ⟺ ∀s: f(xs) = f(x′s).
- (ii) The answer vector Ψ_S(x) = (f(xs))_{s∈S} is a function of the state, by (i). It separates two states iff some s
  distinguishes them, which is the condition stated.
- (iii) Moore's partition refinement gives it. Let ≡_k be equivalence under suffixes of length ≤ k. Each refinement
  step either splits a class or reaches a fixed point. A fixed point of the refinement is the Myhill–Nerode
  equivalence. Starting from the partition by λ (≥ 1 class), at most n − 2 further splits are possible. ∎

**Remark (inert states).** Internal variables that differ between Myhill–Nerode-equivalent prefixes are invisible to
every black-box method. Example: the bracket balance after a prefix has gone negative, which in our program is
followed by output 0 forever. This is a property of the function, not of the battery.

**Remark (design without labels).** Choosing S greedily to maximise the number of distinct answer vectors over a set of
prefixes uses only f, so it uses no state labels. It is a greedy set-cover heuristic for the separation condition in
(ii).

## A.3 Behavioural sufficiency (Proposition 3)

**Assumption A1.** τ ⟂ φ(X) | X. This holds whenever φ(x) is a deterministic function of x and the weights, and the
decoding randomness that generates τ is drawn independently after φ(x) is computed.

**Statement.**
(i) For every strictly proper scoring rule S and every measurable f: E S(π*(X), Y) ≥ E S(f(φ(X)), Y), with equality iff
    f(φ(X)) = π*(X) a.s.
(ii) For every threshold rule on f(φ(X)) with false-positive rate α, there is a threshold rule on π*(X) (randomized at
     ties) with false-positive rate α and true-positive rate at least as large. So the ROC curve of π* dominates, and
     AUROC(π*) ≥ AUROC(f∘φ).
(iii) (i) and (ii) hold conditionally on any stratum s(X), and so for the pair-weighted within-stratum AUROC.

**Proof.**
- (i) Let Z = φ(X). By A1 and the tower property, P(Y = 1 | Z) = E[π*(X) | Z]. For a strictly proper S, the expected
  score of a report r given information 𝒢 is maximised by r = P(Y = 1 | 𝒢). Take 𝒢 = σ(X) ⊇ σ(Z). Then
  E S(π*(X), Y) = E max_r E[S(r, Y) | X] ≥ E E[S(f(Z), Y) | X] = E S(f(Z), Y).
- (ii) Any test based on Z is a test based on X (it is measurable with respect to σ(X)). By the Neyman–Pearson lemma,
  among all tests based on X at level α, the most powerful one thresholds the likelihood ratio
  Λ(x) = dP(x | Y = 1)/dP(x | Y = 0) (randomized at ties). By Bayes' rule,
  Λ(x) = [π*(x)/(1 − π*(x))] · [P(Y = 0)/P(Y = 1)], a strictly increasing function of π*(x). So thresholding π*
  attains the maximal power at every level, and the ROC curve of π* dominates. AUROC is the area under the ROC curve,
  so it is dominated too.
- (iii) Apply (i) and (ii) to the conditional law given s(X) = s. The pair-weighted within-stratum AUROC is
  Σ_s w_s AUROC_s with w_s = n⁺_s n⁻_s / Σ_{s'} n⁺_{s'} n⁻_{s'}, the same weights for every forecaster. A weighted sum
  of dominated terms is dominated. ∎

**Finite rollouts (Proposition 3, last clause).** Let π̂_K(x) = (1/K) Σ_k Y_k with Y_k i.i.d. Bernoulli(π*(x)) given
x. Take a pair (x⁺, x⁻) with Δ = π*(x⁺) − π*(x⁻) > 0. The differences D_k = Y_k⁺ − Y_k⁻ are i.i.d. in [−1, 1] with mean
Δ. By Hoeffding's inequality, P(π̂_K(x⁺) − π̂_K(x⁻) ≤ 0) = P(D̄ − Δ ≤ −Δ) ≤ exp(−2K²Δ² / (K · 2²)) = exp(−KΔ²/2).
The AUROC of π̂_K counts misordered pairs (ties at ½), so in expectation over the rollouts
AUROC(π̂_K) ≥ AUROC(π*) − E_pairs[exp(−KΔ²/2)], where the expectation runs over the positive–negative pairs that π*
orders correctly. ∎

**Corollary (one-step labels).** If Y = 1{next token ∈ A}, then π*(x) = Σ_{a∈A} p(a | x), which one logprob call
returns exactly when A is inside the returned top-k. By (i) and (ii), no probe can beat it.

## A.4 Selection (Proposition 4)

**Statement.** Let S ∈ {0, 1} be an inclusion indicator for evaluation units. Suppose S = s(X, ξ) where ξ is
independent of (X, τ) (past-only selection with independent randomization). Then P(Y = 1 | X, S = 1) = π*(X), and
Proposition 3 holds on the selected units. If instead S depends on τ, then in general P(Y = 1 | X, S = 1) ≠ π*(X).
A forecaster trained on selected units can then exceed the AUROC of π* on those units.

**Proof.**
- First part: P(Y = 1 | X, S = 1) = P(Y = 1, S = 1 | X)/P(S = 1 | X). The numerator is π*(X) P(S = 1 | X), because S
  is conditionally independent of τ given X.
- Second part, by example: let S = 1 iff Y = 1 or a later event W = 0, with W depending on τ. Then the selected
  negatives over-represent contexts where W = 0 is likely. A forecaster that predicts W from X separates the selected
  classes beyond π*.
- CAPE's negative units "turns of episodes that never violate" are of this kind. ∎

## A.5 Fisher–Rao coordinates and noise (Proposition 6)

For a Bernoulli parameter p, the Fisher information is I(p) = 1/(p(1 − p)). With θ = 2 arcsin √p,
dθ/dp = 1/√(p(1 − p)), so (dθ/dp)² = I(p). So θ is an isometric coordinate for the Fisher–Rao metric (the arc length
on the sphere under p ↦ (√p, √(1 − p))). For n i.i.d. Bernoulli(p) answers, p̂ = k/n, and the delta method gives
Var(θ̂) ≈ (dθ/dp)² p(1 − p)/n = 1/n, independent of p. For m independent questions, the noise in θ̂ ∈ R^m is
approximately isotropic with variance 1/n per coordinate. So a linear 3D projection P has covariance (1/n) P Pᵀ.
This is the ellipsoid drawn by the viewer for the text-only tier. For categorical answers (Othello), the analogous
isometric embedding is p ↦ 2√p on the positive orthant of the sphere. ∎
