Let the agent police itself

Before a tool call runs, ask the agent itself, in a side conversation that its real conversation never sees, whether the call follows its policy. If it says no, hold the call for a human. You need only the agent's own API: no second model, no training, no GPU, no server. This page does it in your browser.

When it works, and when it cannot. It works when the agent can judge its own policy: asked about its own proposed calls, Gemma-4 flags 97% of its violations, Muse-Glimmer 87%, Qwen3.8 47%, gpt-oss 37%. Claude Haiku 5.5 flags only 5% with a plain question but recognizes its rule misreadings when asked to check each rule in turn. It cannot catch a violation that depends on a fact the agent has not looked at yet (for example how many people are on a mailing list): no judge of the conversation can. For those, check the world before the action runs. Check how well your agent judges its policy on some labelled calls first (section 1).

3. Predict the effect before you deploy

For a gate that holds a call when the agent's own audit flags it, the share of violations prevented is about Σk wk τk: for each kind of violation k, its share wk of your agent's violations times the share τk of such calls that the audit flags. Measure τk by auditing some of your agent's known violations (section 1).
